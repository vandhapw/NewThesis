This chapter consolidates the findings of the thesis, restates the principal contributions of the QASAMAP framework, and outlines the limitations and future directions that follow from the empirical evidence collected in Chapters~3--6.

\section{Summary of Contributions}

This thesis has presented the QASAMAP framework --- Quantum-Agentic Smart Manufacturing for Anomaly Detection and Predictive Maintenance --- as a unified, three-layer architecture that closes the loop from raw sensor streams to constraint-satisfying maintenance decisions in a smart manufacturing setting.

\textbf{First contribution.} Chapter~4 designed and evaluated a Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (T-GCN) for spatio-temporal sensor forecasting on a 100{,}000-reading, 50-machine industrial dataset. The cross-correlation lag-graph construction (50 nodes, 538 directed edges, anchor at machine~40), combined with two GCN layers and a two-layer GRU temporal stack, achieves an average $R^2 = 0.7702$ across the seven-channel feature set, a 12.4\% improvement over a tuned standalone LSTM ($R^2 = 0.6853$) and 8.0\% over a hybrid LSTM--GCN baseline ($R^2 = 0.7134$). Chapter~4 also reports the supervised classification and regression benchmark across XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, HistGradientBoosting, and TabNet on six health prediction targets, providing reference performance for anomaly, failure-type, downtime-risk, maintenance-required, machine-status, and remaining-useful-life targets.

\textbf{Second contribution.} Chapter~5 designed a four-tier real-time multi-agent system that ingests Apache Kafka sensor streams, buffers per-machine windows of width $W=10$, dispatches them through a nine-agent cognitive layer (Monitoring, Correlation, Pattern Learning, Diagnosis, Planning, Action, Self-Reflection, Explainability, Reporting) coordinated by a phased orchestrator, and emits a maintenance decision via the scheduling layer. Inter-phase parallelism reduces total per-window latency from a worst-case $9 \cdot T_{\text{LLM}}$ sequential schedule to approximately $4 \cdot T_{\text{LLM}}^{\max}$, yielding the throughput improvement reported in Section~5.4. The end-to-end streaming validation on 50 machines (Section~5.5, Tables~5.4 and~5.5) confirms that the pipeline closes the loop in production-relevant conditions: the constraints engine forwards 45 of 50 machines to the scheduling layer at the CRITICAL priority level, and the MILP path returns a one-hot- and capacity-feasible schedule for all 45 machines in 36.9~ms --- well inside the 60-s Kafka cycle.

\textbf{Third contribution.} Chapter~6 formulated the maintenance scheduling problem as both a Mixed-Integer Linear Program and a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimisation (QUBO), and benchmarked four solver paradigms on the same instances: MILP/CBC (classical exact), QIEA (quantum-inspired evolutionary), SQA (path-integral simulated quantum annealing), QAOA with XY ring mixer (gate-based quantum, classically simulated and executed on IBM Heron~r2 hardware). The XY-mixer QAOA construction in Algorithms~6.3 and~6.4 preserves the one-hot machine-assignment constraint by construction, eliminating the dominant penalty term and providing a constraint-native quantum path that recovers the MILP optimum at $N=3$.

\section{Empirical Findings}

The fair multi-seed scaling benchmark of Chapter~6 (5 random seeds per fleet size, identical $T$ across all solvers, identical pure-cost metric) supports four empirical claims that are intended to stand independently of any architectural advocacy.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Classical MILP/CBC is the dominant solver in the tested regime.} Across $N = 3,5,7,10,15,20,30$ at five seeds each, MILP returns the proven optimum every time, with solve times under 130~ms. The MILP scaling extension (Table~\ref{tab:milp_largescale}) shows that this dominance persists to $N=150$ in 781~ms, comfortably inside the 60-s Kafka cycle of the multi-agent layer. For the maintenance-scheduling formulation as posed --- linear objective, one-hot assignment, integer capacity per slot --- branch-and-cut is essentially unbeatable at the scales the cognitive layer actually produces.
    \item \textbf{QIEA matches MILP at $N=3$ but degrades quickly.} QIEA recovers the exact MILP optimum at $N=3$ on every seed, remains one-hot- and capacity-feasible up to $N=7$ with a 44--52\% optimality gap (best of 5 seeds), and fails to maintain feasibility for any seed at $N \geq 10$. The root cause is the penalty weight $\lambda_{\text{assign}} = 3 \cdot C_{\max} \cdot T$, which scales with $T$ and produces a rugged landscape that the rotation-gate updates cannot navigate. SQA fails the one-hot constraint at every tested $N$ (including $N=3$, $2^{12}$ states) because single-spin Metropolis updates are trapped by the same penalty barrier.
    \item \textbf{Quantum-native constraint preservation is necessary, not optional.} The QAOA XY ring mixer (Algorithm~6.3) is the only quantum approach that recovers the MILP optimum at $N=3$ in our experiments, and it does so precisely because the XY mixer keeps the search restricted to one-hot states by construction. This is a constraint-native architectural choice: the penalty-based QUBO encoding alone is insufficient to guide the search, regardless of whether it is solved by simulated annealing, quantum annealing, or QAOA with the standard transverse-field mixer.
    \item \textbf{Empirical equivalence on IBM Heron r2 hardware at $N=3$.} The complex-constraint streaming experiment of Section~\ref{sec:complex_streaming} submitted the streaming-derived $N=3$ instance to \texttt{ibm\_fez} (156 qubits, $p=2$, 4{,}096 shots; transpiled depth $801$, $644$ CZ gates). The lowest-cost feasible bitstring among the post-selected $10.96\%$-feasibility ($446 / 4{,}096$) measurement shots matched the MILP optimum exactly at cost $300.00$ for the same all-CRITICAL streaming-derived priorities, the same one-hot $+$ capacity $+$ duration $+$ risk-horizon constraints, and the same pure-cost metric.
    \item \textbf{Genuine quantum advantage at $N=5$ (constraint-tight regime).} At $N=5$ with the same complex constraint set, the problem is structurally infeasible (5 CRITICAL machines all need slot 0 within the $\rho=1$-slot risk horizon, capacity $C=3$). MILP/CBC reports infeasibility and returns a best-effort solution at cost $1{,}170.70$ with 4 violations; the feasibility-aware metaheuristics (Simulated Annealing, Genetic Algorithm, Tabu Search of Section~\ref{sec:metaheuristics}) reach 4 violations each; QIEA on the QUBO returns a numerically capacity-feasible schedule at cost $3{,}107.80$ ($+165\%$ over MILP best-effort) by exploiting weaker risk-horizon penalties. The QAOA-XY hardware path on \texttt{ibm\_fez} at $p=2$ post-selects the lowest-cost feasible bitstring among the $0.24\%$ of $4{,}096$ shots that respect both (C1) and (C2), reaching cost $1{,}186.02$ --- only $1.3\%$ above MILP's best-effort and the only solver that returns a one-hot- and capacity-feasible schedule. This is the strongest empirical evidence in the thesis for a constraint-native quantum advantage on streaming-derived data, and it follows directly from the XY-ring mixer's structural preservation of one-hot states.
    \item \textbf{NISQ hardware sets a noise frontier, not a qubit-count frontier.} The IBM Heron~r2 (\texttt{ibm\_fez}, 156 qubits) execution of QAOA-XY at $p=2$--$4$ delivers measurable feasibility rates only up to approximately $N=5$--$10$ machines. Beyond $N=15$ the transpiled circuit depth ($\sim$13{,}000 gates, $\sim$7{,}500 CZ) drives the estimated fidelity $F_{\text{est}} = (1 - \varepsilon_{\text{CZ}})^{n_{\text{CZ}}}$ below noise threshold, and the output becomes indistinguishable from uniform random sampling. The limit is set by gate fidelity and circuit depth, not by qubit availability.
\end{enumerate}

The honest reading of these findings is that, for the present problem class and at the scales the multi-agent layer feeds to the scheduler, classical exact optimisation is the right choice. The thesis does \emph{not} claim a quantum advantage in this setting. What the thesis does establish is the architectural and empirical scaffolding required to identify the conditions under which a quantum-inspired or quantum-hardware path could begin to compete; those conditions are stated explicitly in the next section.

\section{Limitations and Critical Reflections}

Scientific integrity requires acknowledging the boundaries of the empirical evidence collected in this thesis.

\textbf{Problem class.} The scheduling formulation evaluated in Chapter~6 has a linear objective, a one-hot assignment constraint per machine, and an integer capacity constraint per time slot. This is precisely the regime in which mixed-integer linear programming with branch-and-cut is most efficient. The findings do not generalise to scheduling formulations with non-linear damage-recovery terms, sequential maintenance dependencies, crew-routing extensions, or multi-site coupling whose constraint matrix loses block structure.

\textbf{Solver budget.} QIEA was run with population size $\max(20, \min(50, n_{\text{bits}}/4))$ and $\max(200, \min(400, n_{\text{bits}}))$ generations; SQA used 16 Trotter replicas and at most 500 sweeps. Both budgets were chosen to match the algorithms' published practice and to keep solve times within the 60-s Kafka cycle. Larger budgets (longer schedules, more replicas, more restarts) might tighten the QIEA optimality gap at small~$N$ but cannot, in the present formulation, restore one-hot feasibility at $N \geq 10$, because the obstruction is the structure of the penalty landscape rather than the iteration count.

\textbf{Hardware backend.} The QAOA hardware experiments target IBM Heron~r2 (\texttt{ibm\_fez}, 156 qubits, $\varepsilon_{\text{CZ}} \approx 0.004$). Other backends with lower CZ error rates, deeper hardware-efficient ansatze, or alternative two-qubit interactions (e.g.\ Rydberg, ion-trap) could push the noise frontier outward; the qualitative noise-decay analysis of Section~6.7 should be regenerated against the specific backend used in any follow-up.

\textbf{Streaming generator.} The streaming experiments of Chapters~5 and~6 use a digital-twin simulator that generates per-machine readings with controllable scenario mixtures (normal, degrading, fault, cavitation, multi-fault). The simulator is calibrated against the 100{,}000-reading historical dataset of Chapter~4 but does not replace deployment on a live factory Kafka topic. The latency budgets and feasibility rates reported in Section~5.5 should be validated against a live production stream before drawing operational conclusions.

\section{Future Research Directions}

The empirical evidence above suggests four concrete directions for follow-up research.

\textbf{Direction~1 --- Constraint-native quantum scheduling.} The XY ring mixer used by QAOA recovers the MILP optimum at $N=3$ precisely because it preserves the one-hot constraint by construction. Extending this idea to the capacity constraint --- e.g.\ via Hamming-distance-bounded mixers, projector-Hamiltonian initial states, or block-Lanczos preparation of feasible initial superpositions --- is the most promising path toward a quantum-inspired or quantum-hardware advantage in this problem class. The QIEA and SQA results in Chapter~6 are the baseline that any such constraint-native approach must beat.

\textbf{Direction~2 --- Larger fleets and richer objectives.} The MILP scaling extension (Table~\ref{tab:milp_largescale}) shows MILP/CBC is comfortable up to $N=150$. Identifying the empirical breakpoint at which MILP solve time exceeds the 60-s Kafka cycle --- whether at $N \approx 10^3$, or earlier under non-linear damage objectives, or under coupled multi-site constraints --- is the empirical question that defines the practical entry point for quantum-inspired methods.

\textbf{Direction~3 --- Hardware-efficient ansatze and error mitigation.} The IBM Heron~r2 noise frontier at $N=5$--$10$ is set by transpiled circuit depth, not by qubit count. Hardware-efficient ansatze that minimise SWAP overhead, M3 readout-error mitigation, and zero-noise extrapolation should be evaluated systematically against the noise-decay model of Section~6.7; the goal is to push the feasibility rate above 10\% at $N=15$--$20$ without incurring prohibitive sampling overhead.

\textbf{Direction~4 --- Live deployment and human-in-the-loop validation.} The 50-machine streaming validation of Section~5.5 should be replicated on a live factory Kafka topic, with the maintenance recommendations submitted to a human supervisor for accept/reject feedback. The Self-Reflection and Explainability agents provide the audit-trail substrate for this evaluation; the missing piece is a longitudinal study that measures decision quality and supervisor trust over multiple production cycles.

\section{Closing Remarks}

The QASAMAP framework demonstrates that spatio-temporal forecasting, multi-agent cognitive reasoning, and constraint-aware scheduling can be assembled into a coherent, real-time, end-to-end predictive-maintenance pipeline. The empirical evaluation in Chapters~4--6 substantiates the contributions of the forecasting and multi-agent layers, identifies classical exact optimisation as the appropriate scheduling backend for the present problem class, and delineates the constraint-native and hardware-efficient research directions through which a future quantum advantage could be sought. The framework's principal value, beyond any single empirical result, is the explicit scaffolding it provides for that future enquiry: a fair multi-paradigm benchmark on the same instances, the same metric, and the same Kafka-cycle budget that any candidate quantum approach must respect.
