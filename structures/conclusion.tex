This chapter consolidates the thesis findings, restates the QASAMAP contributions, and lays out the limitations and future directions implied by the empirical evidence of Chapters~3--6.

\section{Summary of Contributions}

QASAMAP is a unified three-layer framework that closes the loop from raw sensor streams to constraint-satisfying maintenance decisions in a smart-manufacturing setting.

\textbf{First contribution.} Chapter~4 presents a Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (T-GCN) for spatio-temporal sensor forecasting on a $100{,}000$-reading $50$-machine dataset. The cross-correlation lag-graph ($50$ nodes, $538$ directed edges, anchor at machine~$40$) combined with two GCN layers and a two-layer GRU achieves average $R^2 = 0.7702$ across the seven-channel feature set --- $+12.4\%$ over a tuned LSTM ($0.6853$) and $+8.0\%$ over a hybrid LSTM--GCN ($0.7134$). Chapter~4 also benchmarks XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, HistGradientBoosting, and TabNet across six health-prediction targets (anomaly, failure type, downtime risk, maintenance required, machine status, RUL), establishing reference performance for each.

\textbf{Second contribution.} Chapter~5 designs a four-tier real-time multi-agent system that ingests Kafka sensor streams, buffers $W=10$ readings per machine, and dispatches them through a nine-agent cognitive layer (Monitoring, Correlation, Pattern Learning, Diagnosis, Planning, Action, Self-Reflection, Explainability, Reporting) coordinated by a phased orchestrator. Intra-phase parallelism reduces worst-case latency from $9 \cdot T_{\text{LLM}}$ to $\sim 4 \cdot T_{\text{LLM}}^{\max}$. The 50-machine end-to-end validation (Section~5.5) closes the loop in production-relevant conditions: the constraints engine forwards $45/50$ machines at CRITICAL level and the MILP path returns a feasible schedule for all $45$ in $36.9$~ms, well inside the $60$-s Kafka cycle.

\textbf{Third contribution.} Chapter~6 formulates maintenance scheduling as both MILP and QUBO, then benchmarks four solver paradigms on identical instances: MILP/CBC (exact), QIEA (quantum-inspired evolutionary), SQA (path-integral quantum annealing), QAOA with XY ring mixer (gate-based quantum, simulated and on IBM Heron~r2). The XY-mixer construction (Algorithms~6.3, 6.4) preserves the one-hot constraint by construction, eliminating the dominant penalty term and recovering the MILP optimum at $N=3$.

\section{Empirical Findings}

Chapters~5 and~6 jointly evaluate the multi-agent orchestrator and four solver paradigms on streaming-derived problem instances under identical $T$, identical pure-cost metric, and the same $60$-s Kafka cycle. Six empirical claims stand independently of any architectural advocacy.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Multi-agent orchestration meets the 60-s Kafka cycle at full fleet scale.} On the 50-machine end-to-end streaming validation (Chapter~5), the four-tier orchestrator runs the nine-agent cognitive cascade (Monitoring through Reporting) with intra-phase parallelism that reduces worst-case latency from $9 \cdot T_{\text{LLM}}$ to $\sim 4 \cdot T_{\text{LLM}}^{\max}$. The constraints engine forwards $45/50$ machines at CRITICAL priority and the downstream MILP path returns a feasible schedule for all $45$ in $36.9$~ms --- two orders of magnitude inside the $60$-s Kafka budget. The phased dispatch and heterogeneous LLM routing, not raw single-call throughput, are what make the cycle target achievable at fleet scale.
    \item \textbf{MILP/CBC dominates the basic-constraint regime.} On the streaming benchmark of Table~\ref{tab:streaming_scheduling} ($N=3$--$15$ machines, basic constraints), MILP returns the proven optimum every time within milliseconds, well inside the 60-s Kafka cycle. For the linear objective $+$ one-hot $+$ integer-capacity formulation, branch-and-cut is essentially unbeatable at the scales the cognitive layer produces.
    \item \textbf{Constraint-native preservation is essential.} The QAOA XY-ring mixer is the only quantum approach that recovers the MILP optimum at $N=3$, precisely because it keeps the search inside the one-hot subspace. Penalty-based QUBO encodings alone --- solved by QIEA, SQA, or transverse-field-mixer QAOA --- are defeated by the rugged $\lambda_{\text{assign}} = 3 C_{\max} T$ landscape that grows with $T$.
    \item \textbf{Hardware equivalence at $N=3$.} QAOA-XY on \texttt{ibm\_fez} ($p=2$, $4{,}096$ shots, depth $801$, $644$ CZ) post-selects the lowest-cost feasible bitstring among $10.96\%$ of shots, matching the MILP optimum exactly at cost $300.00$ on the all-CRITICAL streaming-derived instance with full (C1)--(C4) constraints.
    \item \textbf{Genuine quantum advantage at $N=5$ and $N=7$ in the constraint-tight regime.} At $N=5$ the complex-constraint problem is structurally infeasible ($5$ CRITICAL machines all needing $\rho=1$ slot at $C=3$). MILP returns a $V=4$ best-effort at cost $1{,}170.70$; SA/GA/TS each reach $V=4$; QIEA returns capacity-feasible at cost $3{,}107.80$ ($+165\%$) by exploiting weaker risk-horizon penalties. QAOA-XY on \texttt{ibm\_fez} ($p=2$, $0.24\%$ feasibility) post-selects cost $1{,}186.02$ --- only $1.3\%$ above MILP best-effort and the only solver returning a one-hot- and capacity-feasible schedule. The same construction at $N=7$ delivers cost $3{,}069.07$ with $V=2$ violations vs $V=8$ for the best classical heuristic. This is the thesis's strongest constraint-native quantum advantage.
    \item \textbf{NISQ frontier is depth-bounded, not qubit-bounded.} QAOA-XY on Heron~r2 at $p=2$ delivers measurable feasibility only to $N=5$--$8$. At $N=9$ ($5{,}390$ CZ) and $N=10$ ($\sim 13{,}000$ CZ at $p=4$), the estimated fidelity $F_{\text{est}}=(1-\varepsilon_{\text{CZ}})^{n_{\text{CZ}}}$ falls below the XY-mixer feasibility threshold and the output becomes uniform-noise-like. The limit is set by gate fidelity and depth, not qubit count.
\end{enumerate}

The honest reading: for the basic-constraint regime at scales the multi-agent layer feeds, classical exact optimisation is the right backend; for the constraint-tight regime where MILP becomes structurally infeasible, constraint-native quantum methods deliver an operationally meaningful schedule that no classical alternative produces. The thesis does \emph{not} claim a blanket quantum advantage; it identifies the precise regimes where it does and does not appear.

\section{Limitations and Critical Reflections}

\textbf{Problem class.} The Chapter~6 formulation has a linear objective, one-hot assignment, and integer capacity --- the regime where branch-and-cut is most efficient. Findings do not generalise to non-linear damage-recovery objectives, sequential maintenance dependencies, crew-routing extensions, or multi-site coupling that breaks block structure.

\textbf{Solver budget.} QIEA: population $\max(20,\min(50, n_{\text{bits}}/4))$, generations $\max(200,\min(400, n_{\text{bits}}))$. SQA: $16$ replicas, $\leq 500$ sweeps. Budgets match published practice and the $60$-s Kafka cycle. Larger budgets may tighten QIEA's small-$N$ gap but cannot restore one-hot feasibility at $N\geq 10$ — the obstruction is landscape structure, not iteration count.

\textbf{Hardware backend.} QAOA hardware targets Heron~r2 (\texttt{ibm\_fez}, $\varepsilon_{\text{CZ}}\approx 0.004$). Lower-error backends, hardware-efficient ansatze, or alternative two-qubit gates (Rydberg, ion-trap) could push the frontier; the noise-decay analysis must be regenerated for any backend used in follow-up.

\textbf{Streaming generator.} The digital-twin simulator (calibrated against the $100{,}000$-reading historical dataset) does not replace deployment on a live Kafka topic. Latency and feasibility numbers should be validated against production streams before operational conclusions.

\section{Future Research Directions}

Future work follows four complementary tracks that together extend the QASAMAP framework. The first track pursues constraint-native quantum scheduling beyond the one-hot guarantee, extending the XY-mixer principle to the capacity constraint through Hamming-distance-bounded mixers, projector-Hamiltonian initial states, or block-Lanczos feasible superpositions, with QIEA and SQA as the baselines any such method must beat. The second track maps the regime boundary at scale by identifying the empirical breakpoint where MILP solve time exceeds the $60$-s cycle --- whether at $N\approx 10^3$, under non-linear damage-recovery objectives, or under multi-site coupling that breaks block structure --- since that breakpoint is the practical entry point for quantum-inspired methods. The third track addresses NISQ depth limits via hardware-efficient ansatze and error mitigation, evaluating SWAP-minimising designs, M3 readout correction, and zero-noise extrapolation against the Section~6.7 noise-decay model with a target feasibility rate above $10\%$ at $N=15$--$20$ without prohibitive sampling. The fourth track moves from simulator to deployment by replicating the 50-machine streaming validation on a production Kafka topic with supervisor accept/reject feedback, where the Self-Reflection and Explainability agents already provide the audit substrate and the missing piece is a longitudinal study of decision quality and supervisor trust.
